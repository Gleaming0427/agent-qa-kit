# How many trials per ticket, 4 November 2026

`node eval/trials.ts retro` and `backtest` on the history, with no model call. The history spans 15 campaigns, the first smoke run included. Five agent versions follow one another, so there are four changes of version. A version's full run leaves out its smoke runs.

## The method

- **Exact over orders.** A version ran n trials of a ticket, s of them successful. What k of those trials would have said is averaged over every order the n trials could have come in. This gives exact probabilities, with no random draw and no dependence on which trial was numbered 1.
- **The conclusion** is the one a release decision acts on. A ticket is "worse" when its success rate is below the latest earlier version's, with the same corpus. In the full runs, 4 tickets got worse:
  - EVAL-1 and EVAL-8 with C;
  - EVAL-1 and EVAL-8 again with D clean.

## Fewer trials

Over the four changes of version:

| Trials per ticket | Recall of the 4 worse tickets | False alarms (expected) | Trials | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 46% | 2.0 | 57 | $6.76 |
| 2 | 79% | 2.7 | 114 | $13.51 |
| 3 | 88% | 0 | 171 | $20.27 |

- **Recall below 100% with 3 trials.** Two tickets of D clean ran 4 trials (the split-runner campaign added one); 3 of those 4 do not always show the drop.
- **Fewer trials miss more, and also raise more false alarms.**
  - With 2 trials, a ticket that is stable over 3 trials can look worse by chance.
  - The two pilots, the same agent measured twice, already disagree on 4 tickets out of 10 with 3 trials.
- **The uncertainty grows as trials fall.** The 95% interval of a version's trial success widens from 13–29 points with 3 trials to 19–42 points with 1 trial.
- **The rules that fail barely change with the number of trials.** The rules on the corpus exposure decide most comparisons of the history, and they do not depend on the number of trials.

## The adaptive allocation

`eval/trials-policy.json` gives each ticket its trials from the history before the campaign:
- critical and risky tickets get 5;
- stable tickets get 2: every trial passed in the last two versions, over 6 trials or more;
- the others get 3.

A follow-up of 3 trials goes to a ticket whose first trials disagree or look worse.

| Strategy | Recall | False alarms | Trials (expected) | Cost against 3 uniform trials | Capped tickets |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Uniform, 3 trials | 88% | 0 | 171 | – | 0 |
| Adaptive | 100% | 0 | 198 | +16% | 23 |
| Adaptive + follow-up | 100% | 0 | 231 | +38% | 57 |

- **By change of version**, the adaptive allocation costs $5.10, $7.96, $5.49 and $4.95, against $3.81, $6.10, $5.17 and $5.18 for 3 uniform trials.
  - Early on, most tickets had been unstable in the pilots, so most got 5 trials.
  - It becomes cheaper only with D clean, once most tickets are stable.
- **For the next campaign**, the allocation is 45 trials instead of 48: an estimated $5.19 instead of $5.73, before follow-ups.
- **The recall of 100% is not evidence for 5 trials.** A "capped" ticket asked for more trials than ran: its replay uses the 3 or 4 trials that ran, and only its cost counts the 5. The history has no ticket run 5 times in one version, so it cannot show what the extra trials catch. The recall rises only because EVAL-1, worse with D clean, ran 4 trials there and is replayed on all 4.

## The risk of fewer trials, whatever the history

Suppose a ticket that always passed now passes with probability p. Then k trials that all pass miss the regression with probability p^k:

| True pass rate p | 1 trial | 2 trials | 3 trials | 5 trials |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 90% | 90% | 81% | 73% | 59% |
| 67% | 67% | 44% | 30% | 13% |
| 50% | 50% | 25% | 12% | 3% |
| 0% | 0% | 0% | 0% | 0% |

- A ticket that breaks every time is seen with any number of trials.
- A partial regression on a stable ticket is missed more often with its 2 trials (44% at p = 67%) than with 3 (30%).
- That is the price of the 2 trials on stable tickets. None of the 4 tickets that got worse in the history was stable when it did.

## What was kept

- **The policy's values are the starting hypotheses**: stable 2, default 3, risky and critical 5, follow-up 3. They are not tuned on these four changes of version, which have 4 worse tickets in all and no strict regression.
- **What would test them:** campaigns that run some tickets 5 times. Their trials would let the replay show what the fourth and fifth trials change.
- **Until then**, the adaptive allocation:
  - saves a little on a stable agent;
  - costs more on an unstable one;
  - spends its extra trials where the history says a change is likeliest.

## Limits

- The replay can only reorder the trials that ran: it cannot simulate more trials than a version had.
- The cost of a strategy is the expected number of trials times each ticket's mean cost per trial in the version; the follow-ups are counted when the first trials would trigger them.
- 4 worse tickets, each by one or two trials of three: the recall figures rest on very few events.
